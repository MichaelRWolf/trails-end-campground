# Decisions Are More Than the Final Decision: A Case Study in Decision Design

## Raw Rambling

The registration system project surfaced something I've been sitting with: people talk about "the decision" as if it's a singular, binary thing. But decisions are actually multi-dimensional. There's a real difference between *who* decides, *when* they decide, *with what information*, and *how* -- and you can decouple these in ways that preserve autonomy, options, and human judgment.

The old system worked for 14 years because it was radically simple: cash box on honor system, unwritten culture. People self-organized. It broke down not because the rule was bad but because context changed -- more people, seasonal rhythm got compressed, cash logistics got messy, and suddenly everyone was asking "wait, who decides this?"

When you try to formalize it, you hit these questions immediately:

- Does the office make all decisions top-down? (Bureaucratic, kills autonomy, doesn't reflect what actually happens on-site)
- Does each staff person handle it their own way? (Decentralized, adaptive, but creates chaos and resentment when neighbors experience different rules)
- Does the community vote? (Participatory, but slow and feels corporate for a 30-person place)
- Can we separate who *proposes* from who *decides* from who *executes*? (Maybe. Different people might be better at different parts.)

What I notice is that real decisions aren't all-or-nothing. Some decisions can be:

- Made earlier or later (timing)
- Made by someone on-site or someone remote (location of judgment)
- Made with local knowledge or centralized policy (information source)
- Made reversibly or irreversibly (commitment depth)
- Made by one person or consensus (authority structure)

And the *same decision* might be handled differently depending on context. A question about "can we accommodate a special request?" might need on-site judgment (staff sees the person, feels the situation) but a question about "what's the official policy?" might need centralized consistency (property standards, insurance, operations).

The heuristics that seem to matter:

- **Preserve options** -- don't close doors early. A decision to "gather more information" or "try it for a month" is better than forcing a binary now.
- **Prefer individual autonomous choice** over institutional bureaucratic choice. People handle autonomy better when they can feel agency.
- **Prefer on-site, in-the-flesh judgment** over remote policy. The person who can see the situation, talk to the people involved, feel the context -- that's where good judgment lives.
- **Prefer human senses and feelings** alongside data. Not *instead* of data, but the human stuff matters: does this feel right? Do people feel heard? Does it fit the culture?

So the real question isn't "what's the rule?" but "how do we structure the *process* of deciding so that the right people decide at the right time with the right information, in a way that preserves autonomy and doesn't corrupt the culture?"

## Three Core Attributes: What Changed

**Old System** (arrival-time, on-site):

- Capacity check → Payment → Site Selection (all happen together, in person, right when you arrive)
- Everything is conditional and reversible until you commit
- Vacant = Available (no gap between map and terrain)

**New System** (planning-time, online):

- All three actions moved offsite before arrival
- Capacity is guaranteed (peace of mind, no risk of showing up to no vacancy)
- Site is pre-selected and locked in
- Payment happens before arrival

The move solved one problem: **The peace-of-mind problem.** Under the old system, if you arrived and found no vacancy, you'd invested hours (or days) driving and have no other options. The new system eliminates that risk.

But it created another.

## The Three Stories

### Jan and Her Dog

Jan selected a site online before arrival and paid. Upon arriving, she realized an electric fence adjacent to her site would be a problem for her dog. She saw many vacant sites nearby that would work better, but didn't know how to switch. She walked to the front, read the board, connected to Wi-Fi, pulled up the app to check if switching was even possible, walked back to visually inspect other sites, then back to the front to try to modify her reservation in the app. I saw her and connected her with Stener, who had admin access to change it in the database. Even then, some of the vacant sites she wanted weren't available in the system due to blocking for future arrivals.

**Under the old system:** Jan arrives, notices the fence, and simply selects any of the vacant sites nearby that works for her dog. No app, no waiting, no admin help needed.

### Steven and Friends

Steven did online registration offsite. Upon arrival he noted "The site is sunnier than we expected." He would have preferred one of the visibly vacant, shadier sites. But he didn't know how to switch online, and didn't want to invest the time tracking down someone to help. So he sweated it out.

**Under the old system:** Steven and friends arrive, see the site is sunnier than they want, and simply move to one of the vacant shady sites. They use their eyes and feel on-site to pick.

### Kevin in a Hammock

Kevin didn't register offsite. He showed up remembering the old process. He drove around taking pictures of vacant sites, then went to the front to see which were actually available in the system. While registering, the system timed out but somehow placed a hold on the site he was trying to select, locking him into his #2 choice.

**Under the old system:** Kevin drives around, finds the site that feels right, walks to the front, and registers it. No timeout, no provisional hold, no second-best compromise.

## The Map Is Not the Terrain

Under the old system, a vacant site was available for selection. Vacant == Available.

Under the new system, a site can be vacant but not available in the database. Why? Because it's blocked for a future arrival, or because someone's registration timed out mid-process creating a provisional hold, or because... the database has reasons that don't exist in physical reality.

The database is a model of the campground. Models are useful. But the model adds a dimension of time that complicates present-time selection. A site that is genuinely empty--I can see it with my eyes, I can walk on it, I can feel if the sun hits it--might not be available according to the map.

This is non-obvious to the person on-site. It requires an app to check. Often requires a person who understands the system.

**Local information vs. centralized model.** Ground truth -- what's actually vacant right now, how does it feel, where is the sun, what's the wind doing -- is observable on-site. The database is better at answering "what did we promise to future arrivals?" but worse at answering "what's the best site for this person right now?"

## Murmuration and Emergent Behavior

Starlings murmurate -- thousands of birds moving together in complex, flowing patterns. It looks like complex flock-level choreography, but it's actually the cumulative effect of very simple bird-level rules: align direction and speed roughly with your 2-4 closest neighbors, avoid collisions. No bird sees the whole system. No central control. Yet the system is graceful, adaptive, efficient.

This is how interstate traffic works. People don't get a directive from some central traffic authority. Each driver makes autonomous decisions based on local observation. The system works.

This is how the old site selection worked. Each newly arrived camper selected a site based on their own perspective, preferences, and real-time observation. No central control. Everyone got a site that was available and suitable for them *at that moment*. People had ground truth: they could see sun, shade, space, views, proximity. They could use their real human senses and feelings to know if the site felt right.

Each camper controlled their own choice. The system didn't collapse into chaos. It self-organized around genuine vacancy and individual preference.

## Commitment Timing

Three different commitments:

- **Campground Commitment** -- Does the CG commit capacity to the camper? When?
- **Site Commitment** -- Does the camper commit to a specific site? When?
- **Payment Commitment** -- When does payment happen?

Old system: All three at arrival time, in person, on-site.

New system: All three at planning time, online, before arrival.

The problem isn't that they *can* happen early. The problem is they're *forced* to happen early, even if better information becomes available later.

---

## On Locking and Blocking

A site that is locked/blocked is a resource that is not being actively used, but is unavailable to someone who is on-site right now. This removes options.

Blocking a site is OK if you are on it now. You're using it. You should have it.

Blocking a site is not so much OK if you're not on it. If you've reserved it for "maybe next month," you're preventing someone who is *here now* from using a vacant resource. You're trading present opportunity for future optionality.

A site that is locked but not used is a wasted resource.

---

## On Options and Preferences

There is not an "optimal" or rigid site selection. Most campers have a continuum of preferences. If #1 site is taken, #2 is likely fine. If that's taken, #3 works.

The campground has amazing variety: morning sun, evening sun, all-day sun, no sun. Open feel, cozy feel. Lake views, mountain views, lake+mountain views. Orchard view, farm view. Sites connected for groups, sites isolated. Easy pull-throughs, castle-hill tent sites.

Given this abundance and variety, only a small number of campers have hard constraints (like needing full sun for solar power, or fitting a 40-foot RV). Beyond those, most campers can find a site that feels right from several options.

When you lock someone into a single pre-selected site, you're eliminating their ability to adapt to real-time conditions: how the sun actually falls, how the wind actually moves, how the site feels when they're standing on it.

---

## General Guidelines (Raw)

- Make choices that maximize future options and choices
- Do not make choices that unnecessarily limit future options and choices
- Maximize freedom (maybe freedom is another way to say options and choices)
- Maximize autonomy. Let people make decisions regarding their own experience based on their personal preferences
- Local decisions based on local observations
- Allow maximum choices now
- Prefer choices of someone on-site over someone off-site
- Prefer choices for now versus future

## Story Arc Proposals

### Arc 1: "The Peace-of-Mind Trade"

**Focus:** How solving one problem (showing up to no vacancy) created another (locking people into choices made with incomplete information).

**Through:** The three stories (Jan, Steven, Kevin) showing what goes wrong when site selection moves from arrival-time to planning-time.

**Summary/Conclusion:** The new system solved the *wrong* problem. It protected people from the risk of arriving to no vacancy--a real but rare scenario--by locking them into pre-selected sites chosen with minimal information. It optimized for the moment before arrival instead of the moment of arrival, when people actually have ground truth. Decisions are not just about what gets decided, but *when* and *with what information*.

### Arc 2: "Map vs. Terrain"

**Focus:** The gap between database models and physical reality, and why on-site judgment matters.

**Through:** Why the database shows sites as "unavailable" even when they're physically vacant, and what information is lost when decisions move online.

**Summary/Conclusion:** When decisions move from on-site to online, they move from ground truth to model. The model is useful for some things (long-term planning, avoiding overbooking), but worse for others (reading what site actually feels right *now*). The best decision-making happens where ground truth exists -- where you can see it, feel it, adjust based on real conditions.

### Arc 3: "Murmuration as Systems Design"

**Focus:** How self-organizing, decentralized choice outperforms centralized control when information is distributed.

**Through:** Contrasting the emergent behavior of the old system (each person choosing their best site) with the lockdown of the new system (everyone forced into pre-planned slots).

**Summary/Conclusion:** Small, autonomous choices based on local information scale better than centralized planning when the information is too granular to centralize. A flock of birds doesn't need a conductor. A campground doesn't need a database to pre-assign every site. Trust people to see what's available and choose what works for them.

### Arc 4: "The Hidden Cost of Commitment"

**Focus:** How early commitment removes options, and the difference between guaranteeing capacity and locking in sites.

**Through:** Showing how the new system conflated three separate decisions (capacity guarantee, payment, site selection) and moved all three early, when only one of them *needed* to move early.

**Summary/Conclusion:** You can guarantee campground capacity without guaranteeing specific sites. You can provide peace of mind without removing autonomy. But only if you decouple the decisions. The new system solved the peace-of-mind problem by removing all choice. A better system might guarantee capacity while preserving site selection for arrival time--the moment when people have the most information.

## Dimensions for Decision Design

| Dimension             | Spectrum                                                        |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Who**               | Individual ↔ Committee ↔ Centralized Authority                  |
| **When**              | Decided upfront ↔ Decided just-in-time ↔ Decided after-the-fact |
| **Information**       | Local knowledge ↔ Aggregated data ↔ Centralized policy          |
| **Reversibility**     | High (easy to undo) ↔ Medium (can adjust) ↔ Low (locked in)     |
| **Authority**         | Autonomous ↔ Consultative ↔ Hierarchical                        |
| **Sensory Input**     | Full context (in-person) ↔ Partial context ↔ Data only          |
| **Location**          | On-site ↔ Hybrid ↔ Off-site                                     |
| **Options Available** | Maximum choices ↔ Constrained choices ↔ No choice               |

## The Registration System: How Timing Changes Everything

### What's Being Decided

Three separate things are being conflated:

| Decision                                       | Old System                 | New System                       |
|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Capacity Guarantee** ("Will there be room?") | Checked at arrival         | Checked before arrival; reserved |
| **Payment** ("When and how?")                  | Cash on arrival            | Online before arrival            |
| **Site Selection** ("Which site fits me?")     | Chosen at arrival, on-site | Pre-selected online, locked in   |

The new system moved all three from arrival-time to planning-time. But they don't all *need* to move together.

### Information Available for Site Selection

| Context                     | Information                                                                                                                 |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Online (before arrival)** | Site number, stylized map, description                                                                                      |
| **On-site (at arrival)**    | Actual sun angle, shade, wind, views, proximity, how the site *feels*, ability to compare visually with vacant sites nearby |

The person on-site has exponentially more information relevant to "will this site work for me?"

### What Each System Optimizes For

| Optimization                        | Old System                                    | New System                                        |
|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **Peace of mind (no vacancy risk)** | No guarantee; real risk of showing up to full | Guaranteed; eliminated risk                       |
| **Best site match**                 | Real-time match based on observation          | Match based on pre-arrival map reading            |
| **Autonomy**                        | Camper controls own choice                    | Camper locked into pre-selected site              |
| **Adaptability**                    | Can change site after seeing conditions       | Cannot change without app or admin help           |
| **Simplicity**                      | Walk, observe, decide, pay                    | Online before arrival, app at arrival if problems |

# A Direct Message to Aaron

Hi Aaron,

In the 300 nights we've spent here, we've come to love this place for many reasons. I could go on, but I trust that you know that we would NOT drive 2,000 miles to spend the summer at a KOA! This place has heart, and I have a bias toward action. Wendy also observes that I have a bias for sticking myself in where I'm not invited. ;-)

So I'm taking a risk here because I see something and want to say something--about the new (and old) registration systems.

## What I've Observed

**Observation on terminology:** Registering has three parts:

- Payment
- Campground lock-in (capacity commitment)
- Site lock-in (specific site assignment)

There have only been a few people requesting assistance, but I'm noticing a pattern.

**People like the campground-level lock-in.** The online system gives them the confidence to commit to plans and a long drive. They sleep better knowing they have a guaranteed spot. That's good design.

**People do not like the site-level lock-in.** It forces them to select a site from a dropdown list and come to appreciate that "the map is not the terrain." What's more, it denies them the joy that is part of discovering the campground in person.

## Why This Matters

Online site lock-in denies people the full information and experience they get from being on-site and feeling what it would be like to live there. Selecting a site from a dropdown is like camping at a KOA and riding on a rail trail--it'll get the job done. But Trails End is not a KOA. The trails here have a "Whee factor."

It does a disservice to your guests (and dishonors the love you put into this wonderful place) to force site lock-in without being on-site.

## What I'm Seeing

I've already seen it a few times this year: people discover that the online site selection won't serve them. They invest vacation time diddling with technology trying to find someone who can help, then require staff time to resolve the issue.

**All of the issues I've seen could have been self-serve** (i.e., last year's process) by simply finding a vacant site that worked for them.

- One guest needed a dog-friendly site. She booked online, arrived to find an electric fence next to her assigned spot. Needed staff to fix it.
- Another guest wanted shade. His pre-assigned site was sunnier than expected. He sweated it out rather than invest time in tech-troubleshooting.
- A third guest wanted a site with a specific tree for his hammock. Couldn't determine that from a map. Required finding staff.

None of these problems needed to exist. And none required your staff's time or your guests' frustration.

## What Would Work: A Hybrid System

**Online (as far in advance as they want):**

- Campground-level lock-in and payment
- Reply message: "Please come. We have space for you. We know that everyone has different needs and preferences, so when you get here, you are free to select any vacant space that makes you happy."

**On-site (at arrival):**

- Site lock-in happens after they've walked the grounds
- Find a space → Fall in love → Confirm it's theirs

This gives people:

- Peace of mind (capacity guaranteed early)
- Autonomy (site selection at arrival, on-site, with full information)
- Joy (discovery is preserved)
- Self-service (no staff mediation needed)

You've built something special here. Honor that. Don't force your best customers to pick from a dropdown when what makes this place special is that they get to *feel* their way into it.

The system could do both. You're choosing not to. I'm asking you to reconsider.

Michael

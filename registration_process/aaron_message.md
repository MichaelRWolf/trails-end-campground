# A Direct Message to Aaron

## Thursday morning

You know we love it here.  That's why we're back for a 5th year.

You might not know that I have expertise in the kinds of database-centric systems that support the new reservation process.

This message is based on wanting to contribute my technical expertise in service to a place that I love.

**Summary:**  The new and previous registration processs have different strengths.  The transition traded one win for many losses.  A hybrid process could have the strengths of each process.

**Details**

- **Campground-level lock-in is a big win**.  People have told me that they value the peace of mind that comes from knowing there will be capacity when they arrive.

- **Site-level lock-in is a big loss** in many ways
  1. **Blind decision making before arrival**  Selecting a site from a dropdown list is OK for KOA because they are identical, but Trails End has range. Personal preferences and site arrangement are important, but cannot be evaluated online.
  2. **Lost joy of on-site selection**  Love at first sight can happen with a camp site.  Seeing a site.  Walking around it.  Deciding together with fellow campers.  These are all joyful parts of the experience, that are lost when not done in person.
  3. During stay - lost flexibility.  If a site doesn't work, or a better site is available, theres a big cost of change (to camper and staff).
  4. Time travel collisions.  Future-people block present-day sites.  This prevents now-people from using the site now.
  5. Staff intervention -  Every site lock-in issue I've seen this year could have been solved by the camper.  Instead, the system required them to invest vacation time, and for staff to divert their attention.

The registration process does not have to be all-or-nothing.  While solving for the campground-level problem, both levels changed, but they need not be coupled.   It is possible to have the best of both processs:

- **campground-level** lock-in using the new **online** system
- **site-level** lock-in using the original **on-site** process

I do not want to intrude in your business, but I didn't want to hide the inisghts that my unique blend of technical expertise and full-timing RV living allow.  I also know that I don't have full visibility into all related factors.  I welcome the opportunity to consult on this, or even shoulder some of implementation work.

Best,
Michael

## Wednesday night

Hi Aaron,

In the 300 nights we've spent here, we've come to love this place for many reasons. I could go on, but I trust that you know that we would NOT drive 2,000 miles to spend the summer at a KOA! This place has heart, and I have a bias toward action. Wendy also observes that I have a bias for sticking myself in where I'm not invited. ;-)

So I'm taking a risk here because I see something and want to say something--about the new (and old) registration systems.

I have an issue that needs some staff time. But I also have a proposal that I believe would prevent the kinds of problems that need staff time.

## What I've Observed

In the week we've been in Site 1, there have only been a few people requesting assistance, but I'm noticing a pattern.

**People like the CAMPGROUND-level lock-in.** Verifying that there is capacity, gives them confidence to commit to other plans and invest in a long drive.  This peace of mind is a gift produces good will long before they arrive.

**People do not like the SITE-level lock-in.** It forces them to select a site from a dropdown list.  The map is not the terrain.  Selecting a site is best done on site, not online.

## Why This Matters

Online site lock-in denies people the full information and experience they get from being on-site and feeling what it would be like to live in a site. Selecting a site from a dropdown is like camping at a KOA and riding on a rail trail--it'll get the job done, but it won't give you the "Whee factor" that you get from Trails End or East Bluff.  That requires being onsite.  In the real world.

It does a disservice to your guests (and dishonors the love you put into this wonderful place) to force site lock-in without being on-site.

## What I'm Seeing

I've already seen it a few times this year: people discover that the online site selection won't serve them. They invest vacation time diddling with technology trying to find someone who can help, then require staff time to resolve the issue (or leave it unresolved and less satisfied).

All of the following problems were created by online site-locin before being on-site.  They could all have been resolved by the camper without any staff involvement.

- One guest needed a dog-friendly site. She booked online, arrived to find an electric fence next to her assigned spot, then took an hour of her vacation time and 20 minutes of staff time.
- Another guest wanted shade. He did not take time away from his vacation, but he would rather have been in one of the shady sites that were vacant.
- A third guest sleeps in a hammock.  You can only know if a site will work by being at the site.

## What Would Work: A Hybrid System

**Online (as far in advance as they want):**

- Campground-level lock-in and payment
- Generates a welcome message that puts them at ease for capacity guarantee, directs them how to select a site

> Welcome.  We look forward to your visit.  There will be space for you when you arrive.   We know that everyone has different needs and preferences, so when you get here, you are free to select any vacant space that makes you happy.  We've attached a campground map so that you don't even need to stop at the Welcome Board unless you want to buy firewood."

**On-site (at arrival):**

- Site lock-in happens after they've walked the grounds
- Find a space → Fall in love → Spend the night

This gives your guests:

- Peace of mind (capacity guaranteed early)
- Autonomy (site selection at arrival, on-site, with full information)
- Joy (discovery is preserved)
- No need for an app (or WiFi)

This gives your staff and campground manager:

- No need to mediate site selection issues
- No tech support troubleshooting
- No database modifications for guests who picked wrong
- No need to monitor texts and phone calls worried about being out of range
- Freedom to actually manage the campground instead of solving lock-in problems

You've built something special here. Honor that. Don't force your guests to pick from a dropdown when what makes this place special is that they get to *feel* their way into it.

The best of the new system - guaranteed availability
The best of the old system - guaranteed site that fits them

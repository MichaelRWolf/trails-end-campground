# Registration System Proposal: Hybrid Model

## Executive Summary

The old registration system excelled at one thing: letting people arrive and choose the best available site. The new online system excels at something different: confirming space is available before the drive north.

**The proposal:** Keep the best of both. Online registration guarantees admission. On-site, people claim sites the old way--by seeing them, choosing freely, and picking the best available.

---

## The Core Problem: Three Coupled Concerns

The current online system locks together three distinct decisions:

1. **Payment** (necessary, online only)
2. **Availability confirmation** (when can I come?)
3. **Site selection** (which site is mine?)

These should be decoupled. Payment and availability happen online. Site selection happens on-site, when you can actually see and evaluate.

## Why This Matters: Bridging Two Worlds

Campers live in two contexts--and two different kinds of time and presence.

**Downstate (Online World)**

- Internet, cell service, connectivity
- Need to plan and confirm before the drive
- Looking for assurance: "Can I come? Is there space?"
- Commitment happens here: payment, booking confirmation
- Time: Future, abstract, maybe

**Upper Peninsula (Outdoor World)**

- No cell service, spotty WiFi
- Choosing with eyes, not apps
- Making informed decisions about where to camp
- Conversations in the car: shady or sunny? View or secluded? Near people or solitude?
- Time: Now, present, embodied, real

**The injustice of the current system:** A 90-day loyal camper gets told "here's what nobody else wanted" while a casual 1-night reservation (who may not even show up) gets first pick. On-site guests watch their preferred sites sit empty, locked out by people clicking from downstate who may never arrive. This is disrespectful to presence, to commitment, to investment.

**The practical problem:** You cannot know what you want from a dropdown list. Wooded vs. lake view vs. open field isn't a logical choice--it's embodied. It requires being there, looking, feeling. Everyone discovers this too late: the site looked fine online, but now that I see it in person, this is much better (or worse). Meanwhile, someone else's clickey-clickey choice has locked them out of what would actually work for them.

**The hybrid system bridges this:** **Online (future, abstract): "Yes, we have space for you. You're confirmed."** **On-site (now, embodied): "You see the land. You pick the best available."**

No one feels passed over. No one watches a better site sit blocked by a no-show. On-site presence and real commitment earn respect--naturally, not through rules.

## The Visceral Problem with Online Site Selection

Forcing site selection before arrival strips dignity from the experience:

- **Arbitrary choices from no knowledge:** Most people have never been here. A dropdown list is not terrain. Selecting a site this way is like choosing a song by reading an architecture description--you're using the wrong sense entirely.
- **Can't see what you want:** You can't know if you want wooded or open, lake-view or forest-cozy, secluded or near others, until you're *there*, looking. Everyone discovers this too late: "This looked generic online but now that I'm standing here, this is perfect for us (or terrible for us)."
- **Removes shared decisions:** Couples and groups making this choice in the car, hands pointing, discussing light and seclusion--that's part of why you're here. Doing it alone, online, weeks prior, kills that.
- **Injustice: Presence loses to clickey-clickey:** You arrive, you're here, you look around and see a great site--but it's locked out by someone who clicked from downstate and may not even show up. You watch it sit empty. This slight happens to every camper, every arrival. It teaches: "Your eyes don't matter here. Your presence doesn't matter. Someone else's website reservation matters more."
- **Locks people in:** If you pick Site 7 in July, but Site 3 (your actual preference) opens up in August, you're stuck. You live with a mediocre choice because your better choice is blocked by someone who isn't here.
- **Punishes loyalty:** Long-term campers (300+ days, 5 seasons) end up with leftovers--"here's what nobody else wanted"--while a stranger's 1-night reservation gets their first choice (which they may never claim). Real investment, real presence, loses to hypothetical future maybe.
- **No-show blocking:** A casual reservation with 25% show-up probability can block a passionate camper from a great site for weeks or months.

## The Hybrid Flow

### Registration (Online, Downstate)

1. Camper registers dates and pays online
2. Receives confirmation: **"You're registered for [dates]. You have a guaranteed site."**
3. No site assignment, no site selection
4. Clear, simple, low friction

### Arrival (On-site, Upper Peninsula)

1. Camper arrives, checks in with staff
2. Drives campground, looks at available sites
3. Selects the best available site they see
4. Claims it (verbal confirmation or sign-in)
5. Can relocate freely during stay if better site opens

### Operations

- Staff maintains real-time availability (what's claimed vs. unclaimed)
- No staff involvement in site selection or moves
- Seniority and on-site participation naturally reward early arrivals
- No app required for site selection; happens in person

## What Owners Gain

- **Predictability:** Revenue confirmed online before arrival (financial certainty)
- **Reduced friction:** No site selection calls, no complaints about "what was available," no staff mediation
- **Cultural alignment:** Respects that people want to be outdoors, not on their phones; welcomes embodied presence
- **Guest dignity preserved:** Loyal guests aren't relegated to leftovers; everyone earns their site by showing up. No one feels disrespected or passed over.
- **Reputation:** Known for respecting presence and participation, not punishing loyalty. Guests talk about this--it shapes how people feel about the place.
- **Freedom and self-governance:** Campers earn their experience by being present; no need for staff enforcement or selection mediation
- **No no-show blocking:** Sites reserved but not claimed free up for people who actually arrive and participate
- **Alignment with values:** The campground operates on trust and presence, not rules and surveillance

## Summary

**Old system:** Great site selection (you see what you want), poor availability confirmation (uncertainty before the drive).
**New system:** Great availability confirmation (you know you can come), poor site selection (forced to choose blind, presence punished, loyalty disrespected).
**Hybrid:** Both. And with dignity.

The difference is **when** and **where** the choice happens--and **who** gets to make it:

- **Online:** Confirm payment and admission (necessary, abstract, future-oriented)
- **On-site:** Select the best available (embodied, now, presence-based, earned through being there)

This way, everyone who arrives gets the best site available to them--not what's left over after website lockouts. On-site presence and real commitment earn respect, naturally and without rules.
